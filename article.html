5 Signs Your Water Heater is About to Burst (And How to Stop It)

Mike R.
• 5 min read • Updated Oct 24, 2024

In this article

1. Rusty Water 2. Rumbling Noises 3. Moisture / Leaks 4. Cold Showers Conclusion

Most homeowners don't think about their water heater until they step into an ice-cold shower. But usually, your heater gives you plenty of warning signs before it fails catastrophically.

Ignoring these signs can lead to a burst tank, which pumps hundreds of gallons of water into your home, causing thousands in damage. Here are the 5 red flags you need to watch for.

1. Rusty or Muddy Water

If hot water comes out of your tap looking brown or metallic, but the cold water is clear, your water heater is rusting from the inside out. Once the steel tank rusts through, a leak is inevitable.

The Fix: If you catch it early, we might be able to replace the sacrificial anode rod. If the tank is already compromised, you need a replacement immediately.

2. Rumbling and Popping Sounds

Does your water heater sound like it's making popcorn? That is the sound of sediment hardening at the bottom of the tank. The heater has to work twice as hard to heat water through that layer of rock, which can crack the tank.

Hearing strange noises?

3. Moisture Around the Base

If you see water pooling around the bottom of the tank, use a flashlight to check where it's coming from. If it's a fitting, we can tighten it. If the tank itself is weeping water, it has a fracture.

Warning: A fractured tank is a ticking time bomb. Turn off the water supply to the heater immediately and call a pro.

4. Running Out of Hot Water Fast

Did your showers used to last 20 minutes, but now they go cold in 10? One of your heating elements (in electric units) may have burned out, or the dip tube might be broken.

When to Replace vs. Repair

The general rule of thumb is the "50% Rule." If the repair costs more than 50% of a new unit, or if the unit is over 10 years old, replacement is the smarter financial move. Modern units are also up to 40% more efficient, so they pay for themselves in utility savings.
